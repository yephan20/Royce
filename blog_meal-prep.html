MEAL PREP VS. COOKING FRESH

September 26, 2024

By Cat Pedrick-Graybeck

SHARE THIS POST

When it comes to eating healthy, how you prepare your meals can be just as important as what you eat. Some people prefer meal prepping on the weekend so they have nutritious meals ready for the week ahead, while others enjoy cooking fresh every day. Both approaches have their benefits, and the best choice depends on your schedule, lifestyle, and personal preferences.

Meal prep can save time, reduce food waste, and make it easier to stick to your nutrition goals. Cooking fresh, on the other hand, offers flexibility, variety, and the enjoyment of preparing meals as you go. So, which approach is better? Let's look at the advantages of each.

The Benefits of Meal Prep
  • Saves time: Preparing several meals in one session means less cooking and cleanup during busy weekdays.
  • Makes healthy choices easier: Having balanced meals ready to go can help you avoid relying on takeout or convenience foods when you're short on time.
  • Helps manage portions: Dividing meals into individual containers makes it easier to plan portions and maintain consistent eating habits.
  • Reduces food waste: Planning meals around ingredients you already have can help you use groceries more efficiently.
The Benefits of Cooking Fresh
  • More variety: You can change your meals daily instead of eating the same dishes several times a week.
  • Greater flexibility: It's easier to adjust ingredients, seasonings, and portion sizes to suit your preferences.
  • Enjoyment of cooking: Preparing a meal from scratch can be relaxing and rewarding, especially when you enjoy experimenting with new recipes.
  • Freshly prepared dishes: Certain foods, such as salads, seafood, and stir-fries, are often best enjoyed soon after preparation.
Which Approach Is Right for You?

You don't have to choose just one. A combination of meal prep and fresh cooking can give you the best of both worlds.

Try preparing a few staples ahead of time, such as cooked grains, roasted vegetables, and a protein source. Then, assemble fresh meals throughout the week using different sauces, herbs, spices, and toppings. You can also wash and chop vegetables in advance while saving the final cooking for mealtime.

Whichever approach you choose, remember to store food safely. Refrigerate perishable foods promptly, generally within two hours, and use refrigerated cooked leftovers within three to four days. Freeze portions you won't eat within that time.

Find a Routine That Works for You

Meal prep and cooking fresh can both support a healthy, balanced diet. Meal prep offers convenience and consistency, while cooking fresh provides variety and flexibility. Neither approach is automatically healthier—the ingredients you choose, how you prepare them, and your overall eating habits matter most.

Start with the method that fits your lifestyle, and don't be afraid to mix things up. With a little planning and a few simple habits, you can make nutritious meals easier to enjoy, whether they're prepared days ahead or cooked fresh in your kitchen.
