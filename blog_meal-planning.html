MEAL PLANNING TO SAVE TIME & MONEY

April 8, 2024

By Cat Pedrick-Graybeck

SHARE THIS POST

Eating well doesn’t have to mean spending hours in the kitchen or stretching your grocery budget. With a little planning, you can make healthier choices, reduce food waste, and save money throughout the week. Meal planning helps take the guesswork out of what’s for dinner and makes busy days a whole lot easier.

1. Plan Before You Shop

Before heading to the grocery store, take a look at what you already have in your refrigerator, freezer, and pantry. Then, plan a few meals around those ingredients and make a shopping list of what you still need. A little preparation helps prevent impulse purchases and keeps you from buying items you already have.

2. Make Ingredients Work Double Duty

Choose versatile ingredients that can be used in multiple meals. Roast a batch of chicken for dinner, then use the leftovers in wraps, salads, or grain bowls. Cook a pot of rice for one meal and turn the rest into a quick stir-fry the next day. Buying fewer ingredients that serve several purposes can help lower your grocery bill.

3. Prep Ahead to Save Time

Set aside an hour or two each week to wash vegetables, chop ingredients, cook grains, or portion out snacks. Having ingredients ready to go makes it easier to prepare meals when time is tight. You don’t need to cook every meal in advance—even a little preparation can make a big difference.

4. Shop Seasonally and Look for Deals

Fresh produce is often more affordable when it’s in season. Compare prices, check weekly grocery store specials, and consider frozen fruits and vegetables, which can be budget-friendly and convenient. Store-brand staples, beans, lentils, and eggs can also help you create nutritious meals without overspending.

5. Use Leftovers Wisely

Leftovers can be the foundation of another delicious meal rather than something that gets forgotten in the back of the fridge. Plan to repurpose extra vegetables in omelets, add leftover proteins to salads, or use cooked ingredients in soups and casseroles. Refrigerate perishable foods promptly and use them within recommended storage times.

6. Keep Your Plan Flexible

A meal plan should make life easier, not create more stress. Start by planning three or four dinners a week and leave room for leftovers, quick meals, or changes in your schedule. Keep a few easy pantry meals on hand for busy evenings when cooking from scratch isn’t realistic.

Meal planning doesn’t have to be complicated to be effective. By shopping with a list, preparing ingredients ahead of time, and making the most of what you buy, you can spend less time worrying about meals and more time enjoying them—all while keeping your food budget in check.
