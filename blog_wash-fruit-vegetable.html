RINSE RIGHT: WASHING FRUITS & VEGETABLES

May 12, 2024

By Cat Pedrick-Graybeck

SHARE THIS POST

Fresh fruits and vegetables are an essential part of a healthy diet, but before you take a bite, make sure they’re clean! Even produce with a peel can carry dirt, bacteria, and other unwanted residues. A few simple washing habits can help keep your food fresh, safe, and ready to enjoy.

1. Start With Clean Hands

Before handling produce, wash your hands with soap and water for at least 20 seconds. Make sure your sink, cutting boards, knives, and countertops are clean, too. Clean hands and surfaces help prevent germs from transferring to fresh foods.

2. Rinse Under Running Water

Rinse fruits and vegetables thoroughly under cool, running water, rubbing the surface gently with your hands. For firm produce like apples, cucumbers, and potatoes, use a clean produce brush to remove stubborn dirt. Avoid using soap, detergent, or household cleaners, as produce can absorb these substances.

3. Pay Attention to Leafy Greens

Lettuce, spinach, kale, and other leafy greens can trap dirt between their leaves. Separate the leaves and rinse them thoroughly under running water. For loose greens, gently swish them in a clean bowl of water, then rinse under running water and dry with a clean towel or salad spinner.

4. Don’t Forget the Peel

Even if you don’t eat the skin, wash produce like melons, oranges, and avocados before cutting into them. A knife can transfer dirt and bacteria from the outer surface to the edible flesh.

5. Dry Before You Store

After washing, dry produce with a clean paper towel or cloth when appropriate. Excess moisture can encourage spoilage, so many fruits and vegetables stay fresher when stored dry. For best results, wash produce just before preparing or eating it, unless you can dry it thoroughly before storage.

6. Skip the Special Washes

Plain running water is generally all you need to clean fresh produce. Vinegar solutions, bleach, dish soap, and commercial household cleaners are not recommended for washing fruits and vegetables. Washing can reduce dirt and some germs, but it does not eliminate every contaminant.

Make rinsing part of your kitchen routine. With clean hands, running water, and a little extra attention, you can enjoy your favorite fruits and vegetables with greater confidence!
