EGGS - THE GOLDEN STANDARD OF PROTEIN

October 06, 2025

By Cat Pedrick-Graybeck

SHARE THIS POST

Eggs have long been considered one of the best sources of high-quality protein, and for good reason. They are affordable, easy to prepare, and packed with essential nutrients that support overall health. Whether you enjoy them scrambled for breakfast, boiled as a snack, or baked into your favorite recipes, eggs are one of the most versatile foods you can keep in your kitchen.

One large egg provides approximately 6 grams of protein and all nine essential amino acids, making it a complete protein source. Eggs also contain important nutrients, including vitamin B12, vitamin D, selenium, and choline, which plays a role in brain function and cell health. While egg whites provide most of the protein, the yolk contains many of the egg's vitamins, minerals, and healthy fats.

Why Are Eggs Such a Great Source of Protein?
  • Complete protein: Eggs provide all nine essential amino acids the body needs from food.
  • Nutrient-rich: In addition to protein, eggs supply vitamins and minerals that support normal body functions.
  • Budget-friendly: Compared with many other animal protein sources, eggs are often an affordable option.
  • Easy to prepare: They can be boiled, poached, scrambled, fried, or incorporated into countless recipes.
Egg Whites vs. Egg Yolks
  • Egg whites: Provide most of the egg's protein with very little fat and relatively few calories.
  • Egg yolks: - Contain vitamins A, D, and B12, along with choline, selenium, and other nutrients. They also contain most of the egg's fat.

For many people, eating the whole egg offers a broader range of nutrients than eating egg whites alone. However, individual dietary needs vary, particularly for people managing cholesterol levels or specific medical conditions.

Simple Ways to Add More Eggs to Your Diet

1. Start your morning with eggs. Scramble or poach eggs and serve them with whole-grain toast and vegetables for a balanced breakfast.

2. Prepare hard-boiled eggs. Keep them refrigerated for a convenient snack or an easy addition to salads.

3. Add eggs to lunch. Sliced hard-boiled eggs work well in sandwiches, wraps, and grain bowls.

4. Make a vegetable omelet. Combine eggs with spinach, mushrooms, tomatoes, or peppers for a flavorful meal.

5. Use eggs in cooking and baking. Eggs add structure, moisture, and protein to dishes ranging from pancakes to casseroles.
