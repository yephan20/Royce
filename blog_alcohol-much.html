HOW MUCH ALCOHOL IS TOO MUCH?

April 24, 2024

By Cat Pedrick-Graybeck

SHARE THIS POST

Whether it's a glass of wine with dinner, a beer after work, or a cocktail at a social gathering, alcohol is part of many people's routines. But how much is too much? Understanding alcohol consumption can help you make informed decisions about your health and recognize when your drinking habits may be putting you at risk.

The important thing to remember is that drinking less alcohol generally means a lower risk of alcohol-related harm. Here's what to know about alcohol intake and how to make safer choices.

What Counts as One Standard Drink?

A standard drink contains approximately 0.6 fluid ounces, or 14 grams, of pure alcohol. Stronger beers, large wine pours, and generous cocktails may contain two or more standard drinks. Knowing your serving sizes can help you better understand how much you're actually drinking.

  • Beer: 12 ounces of regular beer at 5% alcohol by volume (ABV).
  • Wine: 5 ounces of wine at 12% ABV.
  • Distilled spirits: 1.5 ounces of 80-proof liquor, such as vodka, whiskey, or gin.
How Much Alcohol Is Considered Moderate Drinking?

According to the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), moderate drinking for adults of legal drinking age is defined as:

  • Women: One standard drink or less per day.
  • Men: Two standard drinks or less per day.

These amounts are upper limits for moderate drinking, not recommendations to start drinking or consume alcohol every day. Even moderate alcohol consumption can increase certain health risks compared with not drinking at all. It's also important to understand that these daily limits don't cancel out heavy drinking on other days. Drinking less—or not drinking—is associated with lower alcohol-related health risks.

Drinking patterns can become risky when you consume several drinks in a short period or regularly exceed recommended limits. The CDC identifies the following patterns as excessive drinking:

  • Binge drinking: Typically four or more drinks for women or five or more for men on one occasion.
  • Heavy drinking: Eight or more drinks per week for women or 15 or more per week for men.
  • Drinking during pregnancy: There is no known safe amount of alcohol during pregnancy.
  • Underage drinking: Alcohol use by anyone younger than 21 carries health and safety risks.
How Can Alcohol Affect Your Health?
  • Sleep quality: Alcohol may make you feel sleepy initially, but it can disrupt sleep later in the night.
  • Liver health: Excessive drinking can damage the liver and contribute to liver disease.
  • Heart health: Alcohol consumption can increase the risk of high blood pressure and other cardiovascular problems.
  • Cancer risk: Alcohol consumption increases the risk of several cancers, including breast, liver, colorectal, and mouth cancers.
  • Mental well-being: Alcohol can affect mood, judgment, and mental health.
Simple Ways to Cut Back on Alcohol

2. Set a limit before you start Decide how much, if any, you plan to drink before attending a social event.

3. Choose alcohol-free alternatives. Sparkling water, mocktails, and unsweetened iced tea can be refreshing substitutes.

4. Track your drinks. Keep track of standard drinks rather than relying on the number of glasses or cans.

5. Plan alcohol-free days. Build regular breaks from drinking into your weekly routine.

5. Notice your habits. Pay attention to whether you drink out of boredom, stress, or routine, and consider other ways to unwind.
