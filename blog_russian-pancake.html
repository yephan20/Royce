RUSSIAN PANCAKES (BLINI) WITH BUCKWHEAT FLOUR

March 06, 2026

By Cat Pedrick-Graybeck

SHARE THIS POST

Traditional Russian blini (BLEE-nee) are made with a yeast-raised buckwheat flour batter that gives them a nutty flavor. Buckwheat is not necessarily healthier than regular wheat, and the best approach is to eat a variety of whole and fortified grains as part of your diet. It is not a form of wheat but instead is the seed of a flowering plant rather than a cereal grass. It has been cultivated for more than 8,000 years in Asia and has never been genetically modified, which is why I try to mix in buckwheat whenever possible. Buckwheat is gluten free, making it a healthy alternative for wheat. Today, blini are prepared hundreds of ways—with white flour only, cornmeal, sweet, savory, you name it. And their accompaniments have gone beyond the traditional caviar and is now served with smoked salmon, minced onion, chopped eggs, sour cream, and a sip or two of vodka.

Ingredients
  • 1 package dry active yeast
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 3 cups slightly warm milk
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 cup buckwheat flour
  • ½ teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter
  • 4 eggs, separated
  • 1 tablespoon vegetable oil, plus more for cooking
Instructions

1. In large mixing bowl, dissolve yeast and sugar in slightly warm milk. Stir in all-purpose flour and let stand until bubbly, about 10 minutes.

2. Adjust oven rack to middle position and preheat oven to 150° F or its lowest possible setting. In a medium bowl, whisk together buckwheat flour, all-purpose flour, sugar, and salt. Add yeast mixture to bowl and use a rubber spatula to stir until just combined.

3. In a small bowl using a whisk, or using an electric mixer, whisk egg whites until they hold stiff peaks. Using a rubber spatula, carefully fold egg whites into batter until just combined.

4. Heat a large nonstick skillet over medium heat for 5 minutes (or use an electric griddle set to 350° F). Add a small amount of vegetable oil to the skillet and spread with a paper towel until no visible oil remains. Using a spoon, place pancake–sized circles of batter in the skillet. Cook until bubbles appear on the surface of the blini and bottom sides of blini are light golden brown, about 2 minutes.

5. Carefully flip blini and cook on second side until light golden brown and completely set, about 2 minutes longer. Wrap finished blini in a clean kitchen towel, place towel on a wire rack set in a rimmed baking sheet, and transfer to oven to keep warm. Repeat blini-cooking process until all of the batter is used up.

6. Serve blini with sour cream or crème fraîche, chilled caviar or smoked salmon, and traditional accompaniments, such as finely minced shallots, sliced chives, and chopped hard-boiled eggs. Or if you’re a true American when it comes to pancakes, add maple syrup and butter.
